WEEKLY RESEARCH UPDATE  ·  10 NOVEMBER 2026
Point Cloud Color Constancy on all three PCCC datasets
A full 10,000-epoch reproduction on a rented cloud GPU, and a test of when 3D information really helps
Shirsha Mitra  ·  Student ID 2023521460130  ·  Sichuan University
Summary
This week I followed your advice and worked directly on PCCC (CVPR 2022). I used the authors' official code and their released real RGB-D data, and I rented a cloud GPU (Modal, NVIDIA T4) so I could train with the paper's full setting of 10,000 epochs.
Scale: 3 datasets, 704 real RGB-D scenes, the official 3-fold splits, 48 trained models (about 22.5 GPU-hours).
Reproduction: with the same checkpoint rule as the released code, PCCC reaches 0.82° on ETH3D (paper 0.88°), 2.16° on NYU-v2 + DIODE (paper 2.20°) and 1.32° on DepthAWB (paper 1.05°).
Main finding: real 3D clearly helps on ETH3D (0.98° vs 1.31° without depth; better on 120 of 195 scenes). On DepthAWB (1.77° vs 1.86°) and NYU-v2 + DIODE (2.61° vs 2.51°) there is no clear difference.
Depth quality matters: a model trained on clean depth gets worse when its test depth is damaged (with shuffled 3D positions: 1.77° to 2.36°).
Problem and idea: the benefit of 3D depends on how good and how consistent the depth is. I propose a depth-reliability-aware fusion that uses geometry only where the depth can be trusted (idea only, not implemented yet).
Unless marked otherwise, numbers are the mean angular error in degrees (lower is better) of the final-epoch model, pooled over all test scenes of the three folds.

2  Data and setup
PCCC turns an RGB-D image into a coloured point cloud: every point has a 3D position (x, y, z) and a colour (r, g, b). The authors release 256-point clouds (a 16 × 16 grid of the image) and one illuminant label per scene. I used these files exactly as released, with the official 3-fold splits.

Network: the authors' PointNet-based code at a fixed commit (ddc8770). I only made execution fixes that do not change the maths: a return-value unpacking error that crashes the released code, moving one constant matrix to the right device, and skipping a 256-to-256 pooling layer that is an exact identity for 256 points. Main results use the released output layer (3 channels, summed over points); the paper's 4-channel weighted layer (Eq. 4) gives very similar numbers and is in Appendix A.
Training: Adam, learning rate 0.0003, batch 16, 10,000 epochs, the authors' rotation and brightness augmentation, one fixed seed per fold.
Real vs. flat: real = clouds as released. Flat = each point's (x, y, z) divided by |z|, so the point keeps its pixel direction and colour but loses its depth; points without depth stay at zero. This is close to, but not exactly, the paper's "w/o depth" setting (depth set to 1).
Metric: recovery angular error between the predicted and the true illuminant, in degrees. I pool all test scenes of the three folds and report mean, median, trimean, best 25% and worst 25%.
Two checkpoint rules. (1) Final epoch: the model after epoch 10,000. These are my main numbers, because the test fold never chooses anything. (2) Best test-fold epoch: the released training script keeps the epoch with the lowest test-fold error, so I also report this rule to compare with the paper. It is optimistic.
Checking: every score was recomputed from the saved predictions; the largest difference was below 1e-14 degrees.

What this shows
The full training works. In my earlier 100-epoch laptop pilot (fold 0 only) the DepthAWB error was about 3.7°. With 10,000 epochs it is 1.77° (final epoch) and 1.32° (best epoch).
Close to the paper on two datasets. With the paper's checkpoint rule I get 0.82° on ETH3D and 2.16° on NYU-v2 + DIODE, close to the paper. On DepthAWB I am 0.27° worse than the paper.
3D helps only on ETH3D. There the gain is clear for both checkpoint rules (and for both output layers, Appendix A). On DepthAWB and NYU-v2 + DIODE the intervals include zero. My models without depth are also better than the paper's "w/o depth" numbers on all three datasets (for example DepthAWB 1.86° vs 2.39°), so the large depth gain reported in the paper appears only on ETH3D in my runs.
4  Does depth quality matter? (Step C, DepthAWB)
To answer whether the depth source and its quality matter, I took the real DepthAWB depth and damaged it in four controlled ways. The damage is fixed for each scene, and the colours are never changed:
10% / 30% noise: every point is moved along its own pixel ray: its (x, y, z) is multiplied by (1 + e), where e is Gaussian with a standard deviation of 10% or 30%.
50% removed: half of the points that have depth lose it, as if the sensor had missed them.
3D positions shuffled: the 3D positions are mixed between the points, so each colour no longer sits at its own position.
I tested this in two ways: (a) retrain PCCC on the damaged depth (12 new models), and (b) take the models trained on clean depth and only test them on damaged depth.

What this shows
The network really uses the 3D positions. When the 3D positions no longer match the colours (shuffled), the clean-trained model gets 0.59° worse (95% CI 0.28 to 0.89).
A normally trained PCCC is fragile. 30% noise or 50% missing depth at test time also hurt (+0.19° and +0.21°).
Retraining adapts to missing depth, but not to strong noise: after retraining, 50% missing depth is no longer worse, while 30% noise is still 0.24° worse (95% CI 0.01 to 0.48).
So on DepthAWB the model does lean on the 3D positions, but it does not end up better than a model trained without depth (Section 3). The quality of the depth decides whether 3D is useful.

6  What I learned, and my idea
The problem
The gain from 3D is not stable. It is clear on ETH3D, but on DepthAWB and NYU-v2 + DIODE a network without depth does just as well.
Depth quality matters, and the network has no way to know which depth values it can trust (Step C).
The depth format is not consistent inside NYU-v2 + DIODE. The 258 NYU clouds have positive z values with a per-scene median of 0.001 to 0.005, while the 66 DIODE clouds have negative z values with a per-scene median of 0.12 to 3.80; at the median, DIODE depth values are about 221 times larger. The network sees two different geometries for the same kind of indoor scene. In ETH3D, where 3D helps, all 195 clouds use one convention.
Why DepthAWB does not gain is still open (its depth range, only about 123 training scenes per fold, or label noise). I have not tested these yet.
My idea: depth-reliability-aware fusion (not implemented yet)
PCCC simply puts (x, y, z, r, g, b) together at the input, so good and bad depth are treated the same. I want to change this:
One depth convention: normalise the depth of every scene (one sign and one scale), so geometry means the same thing in every dataset.
Two branches: a colour branch (r, g, b and the pixel direction) and a geometry branch (3D position and local shape).
A reliability gate: every point gets a depth-reliability score (for example from missing depth and from how well its depth agrees with its neighbours). Where the depth is reliable, the geometry features count more; where it is not, the network falls back to colour.
Training with depth damage: random noise, missing depth and shuffled positions (the Step C operations) during training, so the gate learns when to trust depth.
Goal: keep the ETH3D gain, and never be worse than the no-depth model when the depth is poor. I will test it with exactly the protocol of this report. I know that gating and confidence weighting already exist in other fusion work (PCCC itself has a point-weighting layer), so I will review related work carefully before calling anything new.

Limitations of this report
One training seed per fold (48 models in total), so differences of about 0.2° or less should be read with care.
Only the 256-point clouds; I have not run the 4096-point setting yet.
"Best test-fold epoch" numbers are optimistic, because the test fold chooses the epoch.
My flat setting is close to, but not identical to, the paper's "w/o depth" setting.
The clouds are already registered by the authors, so I could not check the registration.
The Step C damage is simulated on real depth: it is a controlled test, not new sensor data.
